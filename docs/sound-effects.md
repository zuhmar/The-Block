# First-person sound effects

The prototype now uses 37 original synthesized WAV effects. Five guns have distinct shots; rockets, thrown grenades, C4 placement/detonation and explosions have their own effects. Reloads play magazine-out, magazine-in and bolt/slide sounds as the animation progresses. Other hooks cover equip/dry fire, punches and hit feedback, player damage, footsteps, jump/landing, chopping/mining, tree/node depletion, wood/stone/metal construction, dismantling, doors, pickups, loot and inventory UI. Only successful gameplay actions trigger their reward/impact sounds.

Use the SOUND ON/OFF button and EFFECTS VOLUME slider in the start/pause menu. Desktop M toggles mute. Defaults are sound on, 60% effects volume. Preferences are stored independently under `street-survival:sound:v1`; existing world and inventory saves are unchanged. Zero volume is silent; toggling sound on from zero restores 60%. If storage is blocked the controls still work for the session.

Audio downloads prefetch alongside the models, with a six-second timeout and no dependency on game startup. Missing effects are skipped without blocking gameplay. Audio never starts before a user gesture. ENTER/RESUME creates or resumes one AudioContext within the click/tap gesture, then decodes the cached bank once. Later pointer gestures retry a browser-interrupted context. A browser without Web Audio continues silently. Pausing, opening inventory, player defeat or hiding the page clears current gameplay sounds; inventory UI feedback can play while its popup is open. Muting or zero volume stops existing voices as well as suppressing new ones.

Footsteps accumulate actual grounded movement distance, so holding movement against a wall or jumping cannot create phantom footsteps. Walking/running have distinct cadence and volume, with pitch variation. City/road/raised hard surfaces use pavement sounds; wilderness uses grass. Enemy shots and explosions use stereo panning relative to the current view, distance attenuation, a maximum audible range and reduced high frequencies at a distance. Player firearm sounds remain immediate while damage retains the existing hitscan behavior.

The complete source bank is 578,588 bytes: 24 kHz mono PCM16 WAV with short boundary fades and per-file peak headroom. Runtime voices share decoded buffers, use a master compressor and are capped at 24; oldest tails are released when full. Effects use original deterministic procedural synthesis, not third-party gun recordings. Regenerate with `pnpm assets:sounds`. The asset manifest records duration, peak/RMS, byte count and SHA-256 per file.

Verification covers valid non-silent WAV data and hashes; settings validation and unavailable storage; stereo/distance behavior; grounded movement cadence; one-context/one-decode lifecycle; voice limits, pause and mute. Browser checks additionally exercise gesture unlock, playback graph output, controls and persisted settings on desktop and phone viewports. Headless audio graph checks do not substitute for listening on a physical device.
