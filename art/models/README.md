# Street Survival 3D actor models

Original, source-built Three.js models for the current first-person prototype. These are real meshes with PBR materials, not billboard sprites or rendered images. Units are meters, Y is up, and forward is −Z. There are no external textures or model dependencies in the exported files.

- `black-stick-player.glb`: rounded, featureless black stick-figure character, approximately 1.88 m tall. Head, torso, arms and legs have named groups. Includes a looping `walk` animation and `right-hand-socket` for a low-ready weapon.
- `street-pistol.glb`: approximately 0.23 m long. Separate `pistol-frame`, `pistol-slide`, and `muzzle` groups support recoil and projectile effects. Includes beveled slide/frame, barrel and bore, an open trigger guard, trigger, grip, magazine base, serrations and sights.

Additional original weapon exports:

- `street-smg.glb`: compact receiver, straight magazine, folding-stock rails, vented handguard and short barrel.
- `street-assault-rifle.glb`: full stock, curved magazine, long handguard, rail and extended barrel.
- `street-sniper.glb`: long barrel, short magazine, scope with lens materials, scope mounts and folded bipod.

Each new gun has a named movable bolt and a `muzzle` socket. The first-person rig gives the support hand a weapon-specific foregrip position. The game loads weapon rigs lazily and renders only the equipped one. All weapons are inspectable and rotatable in the model viewer.

## Approved survivor clothing models

The current player uses the new bare base. The inspector includes all four new survivors, generated by `apps/client/src/fps/survivor-models.ts`:

| Export                    | Appearance                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `survivor-base.glb`       | Oversized spherical glossy black head, featureless black body and rounded hands                                  |
| `survivor-street.glb`     | Burgundy hoodie with lowered hood, cap, gold chain, cargo joggers and ivory sneakers                             |
| `survivor-reinforced.glb` | Black military field jacket, webbing/belt pouches, cargo trousers, combat boots and regular open-face helmet     |
| `survivor-armored.glb`    | Amber segmented armor, graphite guards, heavy boots and a fully enclosed helmet with opaque visor and chin guard |

The bare base and Street outfit now use continuous curved mesh profiles in `smooth-survivor.ts`, replacing the original segmented limbs and box-shaped clothing. The Street model has a shaped hoodie, loose sleeves/joggers, a lowered hood rim, conforming pocket, curved cap seams, rounded mitten hands, and shaped sneakers with visible curved laces. Bare base: 5,848 triangles; Street: 20,260 triangles before the viewer pedestal. Clothing is still batched within limb/slot groups (32 model draw calls for Street). The larger Street mesh is confined to inspection until gameplay equipping exists. Reinforced and Armored now share that smooth bare base and use `protective-outfits.ts` for shaped military clothing, rounded boots, beveled protective plates and smoothly curved helmets. Reinforced is 18,700 triangles / 36 model draw calls; Armored is 22,224 triangles / 43 model draw calls. The military helmet leaves the face open; the armored helmet encloses it behind an opaque curved visor, rim and chin guard. All clothing remains independently removable by slot and follows the same limb pivots.

All four exports include the existing articulated `walk` clip. These are lightweight original 3D interpretations of the approved AI concept illustrations, not image billboards. Clothing meshes are baked by material within individual limb/slot groups. Headwear, torso (including sleeves), legs and footwear remain separate groups; `setSlot(slot, visible)` exposes the black base independently. Slots follow their existing limb pivots during walking. They use the same meter scale and forward axis as the original actors.

In **View Player & Weapons**, choose **BARE BASE**, **STREET**, **REINFORCED** or **ARMORED**, drag to rotate, and use the four checkboxes to inspect individual clothing pieces. These controls inspect the models; they do not equip gameplay armor, apply damage reduction, introduce loot items or modify saved inventory. Armor stats and acquisition are separate gameplay work. The first-person weapon hands and enemy remain on their existing rigs; the player's visible lower body now uses the new bare survivor base.

`tests/survivor-models.test.ts` checks spherical heads, bounds/render budgets, independent equipment slots, moving footwear, open/closed helmet coverage and all four animated GLB roundtrips. `tests/browser/survivor-outfits.py` checks the actual compiled viewer, every slot, all four sets, phone/desktop layouts and reopening.

Regenerate all files from the same factories used by the game:

```sh
pnpm assets:models
```

Source: `apps/client/src/fps/actor-models.ts`. The first-person rig uses rounded arms, gripping fingers and a support hand. Its portrait layout keeps the pistol inside the view. The muzzle flash and tracer use the animated muzzle socket; the slide recoils and recovers after firing. The first-person lower body uses the same character geometry with the head and upper arms hidden.

The menu’s **View Player & Weapons** button opens a touch-rotatable inspector. It creates a renderer only while open, clones its own materials, renders only on changes, and releases its geometry, materials, WebGL context and event listeners when closed. Fixed model parts are merged by material to keep draw calls low.

`tests/actor-models.test.ts` checks geometry bounds/budgets, exported GLB structure, walk animation, equipping, recoil and portrait muzzle visibility. `tests/browser/actor-models.py` checks the actual rendered inspector, touch rotation, reopening, weapon switching, slide recovery and first-person rendering. Browser checks use software WebGL and do not establish physical-phone FPS.

`tests/weapons.test.ts` checks distinctive model proportions, geometry budgets, portrait muzzle placement, recoil, fire cadence, and GLB roundtrips. `tests/browser/weapon-loadout.py` verifies real mobile held firing for each weapon, scope switching, a scoped NPC hit, and simultaneous desktop movement, right-button aim and left-button firing.

## White enemy NPC models

- `npc-lookout.glb`: Easy enemy with olive open vest, gray short-sleeve shirt, backward cap, sling pouch, knee-length shorts, exposed white shins and ivory sneakers.
- `npc-police.glb`: Medium enemy with navy short-sleeve uniform, protective vest, peaked cap and generic shield badge, radio, utility pouches and dark duty shoes.
- `npc-swat.glb`: Hard enemy with graphite/slate tactical armor, rounded guards, combat boots and tactical helmet with a transparent face shield revealing the white featureless head.

All three reuse the smooth stick-figure base with glossy white body materials. They retain independently removable headwear/torso/legs/footwear and articulated walk animations. Their source factory is `apps/client/src/fps/npc-models.ts`. Police/SWAT identification lettering is attached in the game and inspector via canvas-texture labels; GLB exports require no external textures. White body material is separate from the SWAT shield material and dark gear.

Run `pnpm assets:models` to regenerate all NPC, survivor and weapon exports. There is one of each enemy in the game; see the root README for territories and test tuning. Nearby NPC rendering is limited to 38 meters, while AI detection remains 23 meters; far enemies do not perform cover raycasts. Render only the selected model in the inspector. NPCs use the same existing combat and projectile systems, with independent health, targeting, firing timers and respawn state.

`tests/npc-enemies.test.ts` verifies separation, clear spawn terrain, avoiding saved walls, independent damage and tier-preserving respawn, increasing difficulty, white body materials, transparent SWAT shield, geometry budgets and all three GLB roundtrips. `tests/browser/npc-encounters.py` walks to and defeats each enemy using real keyboard/mouse controls. `npc-phone.py` inspects each on a phone viewport; the existing mobile combat check verifies simultaneous movement/firing, incoming damage, killing the Lookout and respawning without losing wood.
